Witness for Mac reads incoming texts on this Mac and sends individual messages that might be kind to the Witness service you choose. That service decides what to keep.

**What changed in 0.2.2**

Setup now shows its steps, makes optional Names and Login choices clearer, opens the Full Disk Access pane directly, and can show the running Witness app in Finder. It takes you to the Witness key-creation page before asking you to paste a key. The setup content can scroll on smaller displays, with the crisis footer outside the scroll area.

Closing unfinished setup saves progress. Checking starts only after the explicit final action when the key and Messages access are ready. The final screen identifies missing setup and opens its repair step. Previously completed setups keep their existing behavior and settings.

**What leaves your Mac**

Each candidate message includes the sender's phone number or email, when it was sent, whether it was iMessage, SMS or RCS, direct/group context and Messages ID. With names on, the selected Contacts name goes too. Your own messages, whole conversations, attachments and address book are not uploaded by the Mac collector. Your selected service processes candidates and decides what to retain. Storage is encrypted, not end-to-end; the operator can decrypt it. Read the service's privacy and optional AI-processing disclosure before connecting.

**Install or update**

1. Read the upload disclosure before installing. If your existing setup is complete, opening Witness resumes its existing capture behavior.
2. To keep capture paused during an update, choose Pause, then Quit Witness.
3. Open `Witness-0.2.2.dmg`, replace Witness in Applications, and open it.
4. For a new setup, create a Mac key in Witness, paste it into the app, and choose Check and save. Allow Full Disk Access if you choose automatic Messages capture. Names and opening at login are optional.
5. Choose how far back to look and finish setup. Existing users can review Settings before choosing Resume.

**Requirements**

macOS 14 or later, Apple silicon or Intel. This is the Developer ID signed, notarized direct-download Messages collector. The Gmail correspondence-reader source preview is a separate, unreleased milestone. The App Store edition is not implemented.

The accompanying `.sha256` file identifies the DMG. Source was developed with AI assistance; Muse Nexus is responsible for the release.
